Shopify, the strategy end

I joined the Employer Brand Marketing team as an intern in July 2021 and was promoted to manager in about five months. I was a pivotal contributor to Shopify's Employer Value Proposition from kickoff to launch, a C-suite stakeholder project. I wrote paid campaign copy for talent audiences across EMEA, APAC, and the Americas, including a Singapore campaign localized from audience research showing that market valued entrepreneurship, helpfulness, and impact. I grew the employer brand LinkedIn following 76% and led the Top Employer designation application. Public recommendations from the team lead and a senior TA partner are on my LinkedIn profile.

PHA, the execution end

Healthcare staffing is recruitment marketing with the difficulty turned up: multiple agency brands, regulated claims, bilingual audiences, and candidates who are chronically burned out and justifiably skeptical. I built the employer brand foundation for CHCA: five research-based nurse personas, from the overwhelmed early-career idealist to the isolated rural lifeline, each with motivations, frustrations, and retention opportunities that recruitment content could act on. I ran multi-property campaigns, including a Google Ads campaign for CLXT roles that produced confirmed hires. And the plumbing fix: I diagnosed why job posts across three agency sites never appeared in Google for Jobs or Indeed (JavaScript-rendered ATS portals invisible to crawlers) and built a biweekly AI audit agent posting structured summaries to Asana so the problem stayed visible until fixed. The measurement layer, a UTM governance convention and a content-to-hire attribution architecture, was designed; implementation was interrupted by the company's acquisition.

What this demonstrates. Employer brand fails at the seams: between the EVP deck and the ad account, between the job post and the crawler, between the click and the hire report. I have worked every seam. Strategy that ignores plumbing produces beautiful campaigns nobody sees; plumbing without strategy fills the funnel with the wrong people.
