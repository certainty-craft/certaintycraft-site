Context

The QueerTech Qonference is the organization's flagship annual event, bringing together 2SLGBTQ+ tech professionals for programming, networking, and community building. The challenge was capturing the event in a way that served both attendees and the broader network who could not be there.

The problem, precisely

Event content usually fails in one of two ways: it is captured but never used, or it is used but feels like an afterthought. The goal was content production integrated with brand strategy from the start, so every hour of crew time generated assets the organization could use for months.

What I built

Media crew direction: a 6-person crew on-site, with shot lists, positioning plans, and real-time coordination so video, photography, and audio maximized coverage without disrupting the event. Content and branding strategy: the 4x ROI came from planning backward from how content would be used: social clips, sponsor deliverables, member communications, recruitment materials, and archive footage. Production, not just documentation: framing, lighting awareness, audio quality, and b-roll that told a story. Brand integration: sponsors needed to see their logos; members needed to see themselves.

Related experience

This was not my first event production. 10+ years of AV technical direction for performance venues: Musée des beaux-arts de Montréal, Judson Church (NYC), The Kitchen (NYC), Festival Phenomena, Festival Fringe. The Qonference was larger scale, but the discipline was the same: coordinating technical execution with creative intent.

Honest scope

I directed the media crew and owned content strategy; I did not manage event logistics, venue, catering, or programming.

What this demonstrates. Event content production is not videography. It is strategic capture: understanding what assets an organization needs, planning production to generate them, and directing a team to execute under live conditions.
