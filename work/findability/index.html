Making content findable to humans and AI

designed, partially shipped

Designed, partially shipped; rollout interrupted by acquisition. The KPIs are the measurement system built, not results claimed.

A findability governance program treating Google, AI assistants, and on-site search as one problem: grounding assets, retrieval guardrails, bilingual parity gates, and ISO 9001 document control in a single spec.

Context

Premier Health of America operates multiple staffing agency brands with public websites, a career portal, an app help center, and a Guru knowledge base. Content lived everywhere, was inconsistently structured, and was invisible in the places candidates actually look: search engines, job aggregators, and increasingly, AI assistants. Two concrete failures made the cost visible. Job postings across three agency sites did not surface in Google for Jobs or Indeed because the ATS portals rendered in JavaScript. And 4 of the 5 GA4 properties were dark, so nobody could measure what content did work.

The problem, precisely

Findability was being treated as an SEO checklist. But by 2025 the question had changed: when a nurse asks an AI assistant “how do I submit a timesheet” or “which agencies staff northern Quebec,” does the answer come from us, correctly, with a citation? No SEO checklist answers that. It requires content built for retrieval: chunked, anchored, disambiguated, grounded in verifiable claims.

What I built

A single program specification covering six dimensions, written so a writer, an SME, or an AI tool receives the same compiled rules. Audience tiering: every document classified T1 (public) through T5 (leadership-only). Per-page governance: a checklist template with identity, purpose, quality gates, and technical SEO basics. A weighted scoring rubric: technical SEO 25%, content quality 25%, authority and E-E-A-T 20%, AI retrieval readiness 20%, multilingual parity 10%. Pages get a number, not an opinion. Grounding assets for AI retrieval: each page ships with a machine-readable companion: claims anchored to sources with SME verification dates, FAQ pairs, a glossary with synonyms, a disambiguation block, and guardrails defining when the assistant should refuse. Bilingual parity as a gate, not a hope: designed so quality does not depend on any individual's French, including mine. Compliance integration: ISO 9001 document control built into the content workflow instead of bolted on.

How success was defined, honestly

The program shipped with quantified guardrails rather than vibes: organic CTR uplift of 12%+ on updated pages within 90 days, assistant answerability@3 of 0.8 or higher on a golden question set, citation rate of 0.9+, hallucination rate under 0.05, FR-EN parity of 0.95+. These are the designed targets. The company entered creditor protection and was acquired before the program completed its rollout, so I present the KPIs as the measurement system I built, not as achieved results. The supporting layer did ship: the rules engine (18 automated editorial checks), the legal risk scanner, the UTM convention, and the analytics remediation plan that brought the measurement problem to light.

What this demonstrates. Findability in 2026 is a governance problem, not a checklist. The same discipline that makes content citable by an AI assistant (anchored claims, disambiguation, verified sources) is what makes it trustworthy to a nurse deciding whether to apply. I build the system once so every page, in both languages, inherits it.
