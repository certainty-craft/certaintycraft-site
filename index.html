the practice behind all of this certainty craft.
the practice behind all of this
certainty craft.

Research a world until you can hear its true language, then build the smallest structure that keeps that language true after you leave.

The structure has a name: Core Content, the governed layer where approved claims live with their source, owner, reuse rules and expiry. Every surface inherits the truth from there instead of improvising a version of it. Deliberately the smallest thing that holds: governance that outgrows the problem stops being used, and an unused system drifts.

Bespoke each time, because nobody's content problem is shaped like anyone else's. A staffing agency's true sentence is not a bank's. Research first; the structure is only what it takes to carry the answer.

Everything here is that practice at different altitudes. A taxonomy, a job post, a help centre, a podcast episode, a score for a dance piece, each is a world researched until its real language shows up, then given a structure it can survive in. Four moves, every time.

01 · listen
Go into the domain far enough to hear how people actually talk. Surveys with schedulers. Thirty interviews on a microphone. A rehearsal room.
02 · find the true sentence
The claim that is accurate, sayable and worth repeating. “Onboarding takes two to six weeks, set by credential verification” not the version that tested better.
03 · build somewhere for it to live
Source, owner, expiry, review gate. Cascade, controlled vocabulary, access tier. Only as much machinery as the claim needs to stay true.
04 · hand it over
It has to run without me. A non-technical team using it daily is the only proof that counts, and why the work survived the company being acquired.
· in depth

How the system actually holds

The layer stack is the shape of it. These are the rules that make the shape reliable enough for a non-technical team to run it without me in the room.

01
Resolution is deterministic
A manifest names brand, surface, audience and brief. The compiler walks the stack and emits one fully resolved prompt. It makes no model calls: same manifest, same prompt, every time.
02
Voice is a diff, compliance is a ratchet
Each brand declares only how it departs from the baseline. Compliance and controlled terminology are floors that may only tighten as you move down the cascade. Strictest wins.
03
Voice can be bypassed, traceably
Sometimes the right answer is to break the house style. The system allows it explicitly and records that a person chose it, rather than pretending it never happens.
04
Four gates before a reader
Preflight for unresolved authoring markers and open decisions, a 24-category legal risk scan, a findability scan, then stakeholder sign-off. Review sits inside the cycle, not bolted on after it.
05
Agents propose, operators promote
Nothing publishes itself. Every status change is a person deciding, on purpose, with the labels in front of them.
06
Built to survive its owner
Designed brand-independent from the start. The company was acquired in 2026; the architecture has since been generalized and still works.
· in depth

AI operations with the governance attached

Everyone can generate. The harder work is making generation accountable enough to use in a regulated industry, where being confidently wrong has consequences.

01
A prompt library versioned like code
87 governed production prompts with YAML metadata, a flat prefixed tag namespace, and a Python regeneration pipeline. You can diff it, review it, and roll it back.
02
Eighteen automated editorial checks
Ten adapted, eight written from scratch for this industry's real failure modes: Quebec Localization, Nurse Sensitivity, De-Patronize, Confidence-Legal.
03
Provenance tiers on every fact
Each claim carries a tier checked against a registry of who may vouch for what. An agent can propose research; an agent can never be recorded as a human source.
04
Grounding assets for retrieval
Per page: claims with sources, FAQ pairs, disambiguation blocks, and glossaries with synonyms, written for the assistant answering on your behalf as much as for the reader.
05
Measurement, honestly labelled
Answerability, citation rate and hallucination thresholds were designed and instrumented. The company was acquired mid-rollout, so these are the measurement system, not achieved results.
06
Policy people actually agreed to
Drafted the company AI Image Policy across Design, Marketing and engineering. The harder half of AI work is consent between functions.
· in depth

Knowledge someone can reach into

A knowledge base is a promise about who gets to know what. In most organizations that promise gets made by accident, one permission setting at a time. This one was written down.

01
Five tiers, on purpose
Role-based collections crossed with T1 public through T5 leadership-only, scaled across a multi-agency organization. Excluding someone became explicit governance rather than an artifact of setup.
02
A 270+ term controlled vocabulary
Bilingual EN/FR, governed against the platform data model so the words in the product, the help centre and the schedule finally agree.
03
Grounded in primary research
A bilingual scheduler survey asked the people doing the work to rate data categories by how useful they were for an actual decision. The taxonomy follows their answers, not mine.
04
Work governance, not just content
An Asana portfolio, project and task model with required fields and quarterly housekeeping. That is the part that keeps a system alive after the launch enthusiasm fades.
05
Information architecture came first
Alejandro led the IA and got the rare thing almost no product team gets: structure defined before anyone drew a screen. I followed his lead and kept building into it.
· in depth

Writing where the words are the interface

In a regulated product, microcopy is not decoration. It is the last place a promise gets made before someone acts on it.

01
A toggle is a consent threshold
Profile visibility in a healthcare product is not a settings control. I flagged pre-toggle access as a consent violation and wrote the UI to say what it actually does.
02
Honest about the wait
The credential check takes two to six weeks. The interface says two to six weeks, instead of implying instant approval and spending the trust later.
03
Structure over drop zones
A credential hub written as a structured form rather than a place to throw files. Resumable two-sided onboarding, so leaving mid-flow is not a failure state.
04
Release notes and executive voice
Release notes inside the product; leadership communications co-written with the CTO and credited publicly.
05
One source, two formats
eSoluguide, a nursing clinical reference, shipped as both EPUB and PWA from a single source, with the commit history to prove it.

Premier Health of America · 2024–2026
flagship

A content compiler, not a content generator

The first on-brand piece took over four weeks, almost none of it writing. So voice, compliance, terminology and audience were defined once as versioned layers and compiled deterministically into one prompt per task. Voice is a diff; compliance is a ratchet that only tightens. Agents propose, operators promote, nothing publishes itself. Brief to published became five days.

Shipped and in daily use by a non-technical team. The company was acquired in 2026; the architecture was built portable and has since been generalized. Adoption was uneven across functions, the honest part, and people had their own work to hold together. So the guarantees don't depend on how fast anyone picks up the tooling.

the practice behind all of this certainty craft.
the practice behind all of this
certainty craft.

Research a world until you can hear its true language, then build the smallest structure that keeps that language true after you leave.

The structure has a name: Core Content, the governed layer where approved claims live with their source, owner, reuse rules and expiry. Every surface inherits the truth from there instead of improvising a version of it. Deliberately the smallest thing that holds: governance that outgrows the problem stops being used, and an unused system drifts.

Bespoke each time, because nobody's content problem is shaped like anyone else's. A staffing agency's true sentence is not a bank's. Research first; the structure is only what it takes to carry the answer.

Everything here is that practice at different altitudes. A taxonomy, a job post, a help centre, a podcast episode, a score for a dance piece, each is a world researched until its real language shows up, then given a structure it can survive in. Four moves, every time.

01 · listen
Go into the domain far enough to hear how people actually talk. Surveys with schedulers. Thirty interviews on a microphone. A rehearsal room.
02 · find the true sentence
The claim that is accurate, sayable and worth repeating. “Onboarding takes two to six weeks, set by credential verification” not the version that tested better.
03 · build somewhere for it to live
Source, owner, expiry, review gate. Cascade, controlled vocabulary, access tier. Only as much machinery as the claim needs to stay true.
04 · hand it over
It has to run without me. A non-technical team using it daily is the only proof that counts, and why the work survived the company being acquired.
· in depth

How the system actually holds

The layer stack is the shape of it. These are the rules that make the shape reliable enough for a non-technical team to run it without me in the room.

01
Resolution is deterministic
A manifest names brand, surface, audience and brief. The compiler walks the stack and emits one fully resolved prompt. It makes no model calls: same manifest, same prompt, every time.
02
Voice is a diff, compliance is a ratchet
Each brand declares only how it departs from the baseline. Compliance and controlled terminology are floors that may only tighten as you move down the cascade. Strictest wins.
03
Voice can be bypassed, traceably
Sometimes the right answer is to break the house style. The system allows it explicitly and records that a person chose it, rather than pretending it never happens.
04
Four gates before a reader
Preflight for unresolved authoring markers and open decisions, a 24-category legal risk scan, a findability scan, then stakeholder sign-off. Review sits inside the cycle, not bolted on after it.
05
Agents propose, operators promote
Nothing publishes itself. Every status change is a person deciding, on purpose, with the labels in front of them.
06
Built to survive its owner
Designed brand-independent from the start. The company was acquired in 2026; the architecture has since been generalized and still works.
· in depth

AI operations with the governance attached

Everyone can generate. The harder work is making generation accountable enough to use in a regulated industry, where being confidently wrong has consequences.

01
A prompt library versioned like code
87 governed production prompts with YAML metadata, a flat prefixed tag namespace, and a Python regeneration pipeline. You can diff it, review it, and roll it back.
02
Eighteen automated editorial checks
Ten adapted, eight written from scratch for this industry's real failure modes: Quebec Localization, Nurse Sensitivity, De-Patronize, Confidence-Legal.
03
Provenance tiers on every fact
Each claim carries a tier checked against a registry of who may vouch for what. An agent can propose research; an agent can never be recorded as a human source.
04
Grounding assets for retrieval
Per page: claims with sources, FAQ pairs, disambiguation blocks, and glossaries with synonyms, written for the assistant answering on your behalf as much as for the reader.
05
Measurement, honestly labelled
Answerability, citation rate and hallucination thresholds were designed and instrumented. The company was acquired mid-rollout, so these are the measurement system, not achieved results.
06
Policy people actually agreed to
Drafted the company AI Image Policy across Design, Marketing and engineering. The harder half of AI work is consent between functions.
· in depth

Knowledge someone can reach into

A knowledge base is a promise about who gets to know what. In most organizations that promise gets made by accident, one permission setting at a time. This one was written down.

01
Five tiers, on purpose
Role-based collections crossed with T1 public through T5 leadership-only, scaled across a multi-agency organization. Excluding someone became explicit governance rather than an artifact of setup.
02
A 270+ term controlled vocabulary
Bilingual EN/FR, governed against the platform data model so the words in the product, the help centre and the schedule finally agree.
03
Grounded in primary research
A bilingual scheduler survey asked the people doing the work to rate data categories by how useful they were for an actual decision. The taxonomy follows their answers, not mine.
04
Work governance, not just content
An Asana portfolio, project and task model with required fields and quarterly housekeeping. That is the part that keeps a system alive after the launch enthusiasm fades.
05
Information architecture came first
Alejandro led the IA and got the rare thing almost no product team gets: structure defined before anyone drew a screen. I followed his lead and kept building into it.

Writing where the words are the interface

In a regulated product, microcopy is not decoration. It is the last place a promise gets made before someone acts on it.

01
A toggle is a consent threshold
Profile visibility in a healthcare product is not a settings control. I flagged pre-toggle access as a consent violation and wrote the UI to say what it actually does.
02
Honest about the wait
The credential check takes two to six weeks. The interface says two to six weeks, instead of implying instant approval and spending the trust later.
03
Structure over drop zones
A credential hub written as a structured form rather than a place to throw files. Resumable two-sided onboarding, so leaving mid-flow is not a failure state.
04
Release notes and executive voice
Release notes inside the product; leadership communications co-written with the CTO and credited publicly.
05
One source, two formats
eSoluguide, a nursing clinical reference, shipped as both EPUB and PWA from a single source, with the commit history to prove it.

Premier Health of America · 2024–2026 flagship

A content compiler, not a content generator

The first on-brand piece took over four weeks, almost none of it writing. So voice, compliance, terminology and audience were defined once as versioned layers and compiled deterministically into one prompt per task. Voice is a diff; compliance is a ratchet that only tightens. Agents propose, operators promote, nothing publishes itself. Brief to published became five days.

Shipped and in daily use by a non-technical team. The company was acquired in 2026; the architecture was built portable and has since been generalized. Adoption was uneven across functions, the honest part, and people had their own work to hold together. So the guarantees don't depend on how fast anyone picks up the tooling.
